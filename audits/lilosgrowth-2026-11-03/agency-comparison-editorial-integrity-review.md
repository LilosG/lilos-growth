## Source-level findings

### Roofing-agency roundup

**URL:** `/blog/top-local-seo-agencies-roofers-san-diego-local-guide`  
**Current title:** Best Local SEO Agencies for Roofers in San Diego: A Practical Roundup

- The main source is positioned as a San Diego agency roundup but its H2s primarily discuss LILOs, general service expectations, red flags and agency evaluation. It does not clearly substantiate a balanced, independently researched list of comparable providers.
- Phrases such as “Lilos Growth brings roofing expertise, proven results with home service contractors” and “Roofing-Focused Local SEO” should be reviewed against actual verified projects. Preserve industry expertise without representing the entire agency as roofing-only.
- Frontmatter FAQ promises a typical 3–6 months to results and 4–6 weeks for GBP quick wins. No evidence in source supports those predicted time windows.
- The article is published by the same agency it endorses, without a clear, early publisher/selection disclosure.
- **Recommended editorial direction:** Either substantiate the existing “best agencies” comparison with independently verified, current company details and a transparent selection methodology, or retitle/restructure around *how to evaluate* a San Diego roofing SEO partner. **Do not retitle until query intent and click-through risk are reviewed.**

## Publication standards before modification

1. **Disclosure:** Explicit near-introduction notice: “Published by LILOs Growth, which provides services included in this comparison. We describe our own offering; readers should independently assess other providers.” Wording must be natural and clearly visible.
2. **Comparisons:** Named providers must have verifiable current identity, official websites and service descriptions; record a source and checked date for each significant claim. Avoid unsupported “best,” “top” and numeric rank assertions.
3. **Evidence:** Do not invent client statistics, impressions-to-leads conversions, fees, rankings, testimonials or timelines. Use permissioned internal case studies with exact claims or state methodology without outcomes.
4. **Intent:** Keep genuinely different roofing, electrician and restoration decision criteria. Avoid one generic template reproduced with industry names swapped.
5. **Presentation:** Retain existing high-exposure URLs and self-canonicals; use descriptive internal links to relevant service pages, `/results`, and `/contact#book`. Do not force the agency’s homepage messaging toward roofing.
6. **Search:** Before changing title/H1 and introduction, review the GSC **query×page** distribution, devices and SERP format. Pages aggregate data cannot identify whether high impressions are agency-selection queries or general how-to searches.
7. **Quality assurance:** Check real rendered mobile/desktop content, links, structured data, factual consistency and analytics events in preview; compare GSC and qualified leads after launch.

## Proposed execution order

**First:** Roofing article because of its highest exposure and “roundup” promise without a robust visible comparative list. Create evidence inventory and revised outline; decide whether to substantiate the comparison or reposition as a selection guide while protecting actual query intent.  
**Second:** Electricians — verify claims and connect to documented electrical project evidence.  
**Third:** Restoration — audit named agency/service claims and remove ungrounded quantified outcomes.
