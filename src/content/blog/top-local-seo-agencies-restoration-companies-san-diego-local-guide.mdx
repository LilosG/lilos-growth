## What restoration contractors need from an SEO provider

Water damage, fire and smoke restoration, mold remediation, and related cleanup services involve different customer problems and technical requirements. A competent provider should learn which services a restoration company truly offers, where crews can respond, and what credentials or licensing must be represented accurately.

Compare proposals in these areas:

- **Google Business Profile (GBP):** Correct category, business name, phone, address or service-area representation, hours, and policy-compliant reviews
- **Service structure:** Distinct, helpful water, fire, mold, and related pages when the company offers each service
- **Location accuracy:** Real dispatch coverage and meaningful local details, not fabricated offices or repeated city-name templates
- **Emergency contact paths:** Mobile click-to-call, after-hours handling, working forms, and clear availability statements
- **Proof and trust:** Authentic case studies, permissioned photos, relevant certifications, and substantiated claims
- **Measurement and ownership:** Qualified phone inquiries, requested estimates, booked jobs, and administrative control of accounts and website assets

Google describes local search primarily through [relevance, distance, and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). Neither a post quota nor a promise of "rapid review velocity" guarantees placement, and reviews must follow platform policies.

## Seven questions to include in every agency proposal

1. **Which services and communities will you target?** Water extraction, fire cleanup, mold remediation, reconstruction, and emergency availability cannot be assumed.
2. **What will be changed on the site and GBP?** Ask for deliverables, location eligibility checks, content approvals, and quality assurance.
3. **How will you verify technical claims?** Require qualified restoration staff to review safety, mold, contaminated-water, and insurance-related content.
4. **Can you show permissioned, relevant work?** Confirm dates, scope, source of results, and whether outcomes were organic, paid, or blended.
5. **How are calls and leads counted?** Differentiate listing interactions, phone-button clicks, answered calls, qualified dispatch requests, and booked restoration work.
6. **Who owns the accounts and integrations?** Clarify the website, domain, GBP, analytics, ad accounts, CRM, content, and data access.
7. **What does the proposal cost and what happens at cancellation?** Separate setup, recurring SEO, advertising spend, software, content, reporting, and handoff.

Avoid agencies offering fictional Google offices, review manipulation, mass-produced doorway pages, or guaranteed map-pack rankings.

## How to evaluate success

Search Console impressions indicate visibility, not dispatches. GBP interactions are useful but do not automatically represent connected calls. Actual qualified leads should be reconciled with the phone system or CRM where feasible, with privacy-compliant analytics and explicit attribution limitations.

Restoration demand can shift due to seasonality and unpredictable incidents. Evaluate several relevant measures together instead of judging a campaign on one keyword ranking or a short reporting period.

## Next steps for a restoration business

Use a common scope when comparing bids, and insist on actual deliverables and verified qualifications. The right partner should strengthen discoverability and customer contact without misrepresenting the company's operations.
