## What Review Management Actually Costs You (The Hidden Expenses)

Beyond the monthly fee, there are costs you need to factor in:

**Time to respond to reviews yourself:** If you're not using an agency, you're spending 1–2 hours weekly responding to feedback. At $50–$100/hour (your hourly value), that's $200–$400 monthly in lost productivity.

**Negative review damage:** One bad review without a professional response can cost you 2–3 jobs. That's $3,000–$9,000 in lost revenue from a single unmanaged review. Review management prevents this.

**Slow review generation:** Without systematic requests, you'll generate 2–4 reviews monthly naturally. At Tier 2 pricing, you're paying $800–$1,500 to get 8–15. That's an extra 4–11 reviews. Each review is worth approximately $500–$1,000 in perceived credibility and search ranking value.

**Lower search rankings:** Without consistent review activity, your Google Business Profile stagnates. Competitors with fresh reviews rank higher. You lose visibility. You lose calls.

The real cost of _not_ investing in review management is usually higher than the cost of investing.

## Common Questions About Review Management Cost

**"Can't I just ask customers for reviews myself?"**

Technically yes. Practically, most contractors don't. You'll forget, customers will forget, and you'll generate 2–3 reviews monthly instead of 10–15. The consistency and automation matter more than the ask itself.

**"What if I get a bad review while using a review management service?"**

They respond professionally and quickly. They don't delete it (Google doesn't allow that and it's illegal). They address the concern, offer solutions, and show future customers you care about feedback. A well-handled negative review actually builds trust.

**"How long before I see results?"**

Lead volume typically improves within 30–45 days as review count increases and your ranking climbs. Full ROI (where the service pays for itself in new revenue) usually hits within 60–90 days.

**"Is there a contract lock-in?"**

Depends on the agency. Most reputable services (including ours) don't lock you in. Month-to-month is standard. If you're not seeing results after 90 days, you can leave.

## What to Avoid When Buying Review Management

**Avoid agencies that promise guaranteed rankings.** Google doesn't guarantee anything. Agencies that promise #1 rankings are lying.

**Avoid services that delete negative reviews.** It's against Google's terms of service and it's unethical. Good review management addresses negative feedback, not hides it.

**Avoid flat-rate services with no customization.** Your business is different from the plumber down the street. Your review strategy should be too.

**Avoid agencies that don't provide monthly reporting.** You need to see what's happening, how many reviews you're getting, and how your ranking is changing. If they won't show you, they're not confident in their work.
